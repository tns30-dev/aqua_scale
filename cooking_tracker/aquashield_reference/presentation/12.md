## Speaker notes (≈ 60s — say out loud, don't put on slide · placeholder, fill in)

> "Two views of the data layer.

> **Left — the relational ERD.** Twenty-three tables grouped into ten domains: identity and access, profile types, projects, ponds, parameter types, growth cycles, sensors, IoT devices, visualisations, and alerts. We use foreign keys for fixed-shape relationships and composite primary keys where the relationship itself is the identity — `user_projects` and `project_parameter_settings` are both `(parent_id, child_id)` composites.

> **Right — the document-shaped data.** Some structures don't fit cleanly into a relational schema because their shape varies by tenant or per profile. Three examples: `users.feature_access` and `users.action_control` are JSONB arrays of subscription-driven permissions; `profile_types.stage_config` is a JSONB array of growth-stage definitions that's different for shrimp, fish, and crab hatchery; `project_visualisations.config` stores chart-type-specific configuration. Keeping these as JSONB inside Postgres avoids a separate NoSQL store while preserving query flexibility through Postgres's native JSONB operators."

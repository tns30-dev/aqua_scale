# Sprint Retrospective

The sprint retrospective covers the completed work from Sprint 1 to Sprint 9. It does not evaluate the planned ML and agentic sprints because those increments have not been carried out yet.

| No. | Area | Observation | Improvement Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product understanding | The platform value became clearer when water monitoring was connected to treatment-effectiveness and business evidence. | Continue writing requirements from the viewpoint of farmers, researchers, product owners, and business owners. |
| 2 | Backlog structure | Grouping the work by epics helped separate daily farm operations, research analysis, business reporting, and platform administration. | Keep backlog items tied to clear user roles and measurable outcomes. |
| 3 | Scope control | AquaShield has many possible capabilities, so completed sprints needed clear boundaries. | Keep each sprint focused on one coherent product increment. |
| 4 | Domain clarity | Several features depended on the relationship between project, pond, cycle, sensor reading, treatment, and alert records. | Define data relationships and acceptance criteria before starting complex workflow items. |
| 5 | User experience | Farmers need quick operational understanding, while researchers and business owners need detailed evidence. | Separate operational dashboard needs from analytical and reporting needs. |
| 6 | Treatment evidence | Treatment-effectiveness tracking is central to the business case but requires accurate before-and-after data. | Make treatment timestamps, related readings, and comparison rules explicit in upcoming backlog items. |
| 7 | Alert workflow | Alert generation alone is not enough; acknowledgement and history are needed for operational accountability. | Include response tracking and incident review in alert-related stories. |
| 8 | Trust and reporting | Platform reports are only useful if the underlying data can be trusted and traced. | Continue treating traceability, audit history, and testing as part of the product rather than as optional finishing work. |

## Retrospective Conclusion

The main lesson from the completed sprints is that AquaShield should be treated as an aquaculture decision-support platform rather than only a water-monitoring application. The strongest product value comes from connecting water-quality data with pond operations, treatment records, comparison, alerts, and business evidence. The planned ML and agentic sprints should build on this foundation and should receive their own review and retrospective after they are completed.
